Singapore Residential Property Purchase Logic Flows (2025)
Property Types Covered

HDB BTO Flats (Build-to-Order) – New public housing units allocated by ballot. Buyers apply during launch and wait ~3-5 years for construction. BTO flats are subsidized with strict eligibility (citizenship, income) and a 5-year Minimum Occupation Period (MOP) after key collection
channelnewsasia.com
dbs.com.sg
. Categories from 2H2024 include Standard, Plus, and Prime flats with varying subsidy levels and resale restrictions (e.g. 10-year MOP for Prime/Plus)
channelnewsasia.com
.

HDB Resale Flats – Pre-owned HDB flats sold in the open market. Immediate move-in with no construction wait, but buyers must meet HDB eligibility (at least one Singapore Citizen or Permanent Resident, family nucleus requirements, etc.). Resale flats have a 5-year MOP for the new owner as well (even if the flat is past its first MOP)
dbs.com.sg
. Certain resale flats (e.g. Prime/Plus flats) have additional conditions like income ceiling (S$14,000) and, for Plus/Prime, only Singaporeans can buy and private owners face a 30-month wait-out to purchase
channelnewsasia.com
channelnewsasia.com
.

New Launch Private Properties – Brand new condominiums or landed houses sold by developers. Open to all buyers (locals and foreigners*) under private market rules. No income or family nucleus requirements. Purchases follow a progressive payment schedule (for under-construction condos) and typically require a 20% downpayment (5% booking fee in cash, 15% upon signing Sale & Purchase) with the remaining via staged payments as building milestones complete. No government-imposed MOP; owners can sell anytime, but Seller’s Stamp Duty (SSD) applies if sold within 4 years as of July 2025
mortgagemaster.com.sg
. [Foreigners need government approval to buy landed houses; no approval needed for condominiums.]

Resale Private Properties – Any completed private residential property (condo, landed, etc.) sold by current owner to a buyer. No eligibility restrictions besides legal age (21) and financing qualification. Standard private purchase timelines apply (option fee, completion in ~8-12 weeks). Foreigners may buy condos freely, but landed property purchase by foreigners requires prior approval. There is no MOP, but SSD applies if the property was purchased recently (see Stamp Duties below).

Buyer Profiles and Categories

First-Time Buyers (FTB) – Individuals or households who have never taken any housing subsidy from HDB. This includes those who have not bought a BTO/EC before and have not received CPF housing grants. First-timers get priority in BTO balloting and qualify for more grants. HDB considers a “first-timer” status per household – all applicants/essential occupiers must be subsidy-free
hdb.gov.sg
. (If one spouse has prior subsidy, the couple is generally treated as second-timer, though a reduced “half grant” may apply in resale scenarios.)

Second-Timers – Buyers who have obtained a housing subsidy before (e.g. bought a BTO or received CPF grants for resale). Second-timers face certain quotas and waits: for example, after selling a subsidized flat or EC, they must wait 30 months before applying for another subsidized HDB flat or grant
buddy.edgeprop.sg
. They may also need to pay a resale levy when buying another new subsidized flat. Second-timers get lower priority in BTO applications (a smaller quota of flats) and are not eligible for most grants (grants are primarily for first-timers).

Singles vs. Couples/Families – HDB defines family nucleus requirements. Singles (unmarried, divorced, widowed) aged ≥35 can buy certain flats: for BTO, only 2-room Flexi in non-mature estates (income ≤ $7,000)
channelnewsasia.com
; for resale, singles (SC only) can purchase any flat size in any location. Singles qualify for Singles Grants (which are roughly half the family grant)
propertyguru.com.sg
. Couples/Families (married or engaged couples, or parents with children) can buy any HDB flat type if eligible. At least one applicant must be a Singapore Citizen for BTO; for resale, at least one SC or SPR (with at least 3 years of SPR status) is required. Families enjoy higher income ceilings (e.g. $14,000 for most HDB schemes, or $21,000 for extended families applying jointly) and can access full grant amounts.

Joint Buyers vs Individual Buyers – Purchasing under two names (e.g. spouses jointly, or up to 4 unrelated singles in the Joint Singles Scheme for resale) can combine incomes for loan and grant considerations. For HDB, all joint buyers must meet eligibility criteria (e.g. all must be SC/PR, form one of HDB’s eligible schemes: married couple, siblings or singles 35+, etc.). Joint Singles Scheme allows 2 to 4 single Singaporeans (≥35) to jointly purchase a resale flat of at most 5-room size. Individual buyer refers to a lone purchaser – e.g. a single 35+ buying HDB resale alone, or any sole buyer of a private property.

Eligibility Checks & Constraints

When planning a purchase, the app must first evaluate eligibility based on buyer profile and property type:

Age Requirement: For any legal contract, buyer must be 21 or older (for HDB, minors can only purchase if orphaned or through trustees). Singles scheme for HDB requires age ≥35 (unless orphaned with siblings). Fiancé/fiancée couples must be 21 and above (or 18 with parental consent) and must register marriage by key collection (for BTO) or within 3 months of resale completion.

Citizenship and Residency:

BTO – At least one applicant must be a Singapore Citizen (SC). The other can be SC or Singapore Permanent Resident (SPR). Singles buying BTO (2-room) must be SC.

Resale HDB – At least one SC or SPR in the buyer group. However, SPR households with no SC face additional rules: all SPR buyers must have at least 3 years of PR status before they can buy an HDB resale. (Also, if it’s an SPR household with no SC, they are subject to the Non-Citizen quota for resale flats in certain blocks/neighborhoods.)

Private Property – No nationality restrictions for condos, but foreigners cannot purchase HDBs and need government approval for landed houses. Singapore PRs cannot own an HDB and a private property at the same time (PRs must dispose of an HDB within 6 months of buying private)
dbs.com.sg
, whereas Singapore Citizens are allowed to own one HDB and additional private properties (after meeting HDB’s MOP)
dbs.com.sg
.

Income Ceilings:

BTO/EC – Subject to household income ceilings. Standard flats and ECs: $14,000 for families (or $21,000 for extended families); Singles 2-room BTO: $7,000
channelnewsasia.com
. New Plus/Prime BTO flats will also carry these income ceilings. (EC income ceiling is $16,000 as of 2023, slightly higher for ECs).

HDB Resale – No income ceiling to buy a regular resale flat except if using certain grants (grants have their own income limits, e.g. EHG up to $9,000 income). However, note that from 2024, Prime/Plus resale flats will impose a ~$14,000 income ceiling even for resale buyers
channelnewsasia.com
channelnewsasia.com
.

Private – No income limits to purchase, but income affects loan eligibility (TDSR) rather than purchase right.

Family Nucleus Definitions (HDB): HDB flats must be purchased under an eligible scheme. Common schemes:

Public Scheme (Family) – married or engaged couple, or parents with children (can include one single child buying with parents).

Fiancé/Fiancée Scheme – engaged couples (must marry by flat handover).

Single Singapore Citizen Scheme – a single SC aged ≥35 buying alone (for resale any size; for BTO only 2-room in non-mature).

Joint Singles Scheme – 2 to 4 single SCs (≥35) buying a resale flat jointly.

Orphans Scheme – orphan siblings (≥21, at least one SC) buying together.

Non-Citizen Spouse Scheme – SC applicant with a non-citizen spouse (who is on valid Long-Term Visit Pass at least); can only buy resale HDB (not BTO) and no grants.

Extended Family – e.g. married couple applying with one of their parents (can be eligible for higher income ceiling $21k for BTO).

The app should validate that the buyer group fits one of these schemes for HDB. (Example: A single PR cannot buy an HDB alone – not allowed under any scheme. Two PRs married to each other can buy resale under Public Scheme, provided one has 3 years PR status.)

Existing Property Ownership: HDB flat buyers cannot hold other residential property (local or overseas) at point of purchase. Applicants must dispose of any private property at least 30 months before applying for a new subsidized HDB (BTO or buying resale with grants)
propertyguru.com.sg
propertyguru.com.sg
. For HDB resale without grants, current rule since Sep 2022 is that if you own (or just sold) private property, you must observe a 15-month wait-out before buying a non-subsidized resale HDB
ohmyhome.com
. (This wait-out is waived for seniors aged ≥55 who are buying a 4-room or smaller flat, allowing them to downsize without waiting
ohmyhome.com
.) In Aug 2023 it was announced that Prime/Plus flats resale will have a stricter 30-month wait for private owners
channelnewsasia.com
channelnewsasia.com
. Summary: The app should enforce that HDB buyers have no other property and, if they sold any, the required wait period has passed (15 months for standard resale, 30 months for new subsidized flat or Prime/Plus resale)
channelnewsasia.com
. Private property purchases have no such restriction (one can own multiple private homes, subject to financing and stamp duties).

Previous Subsidies & Wait-Outs: If the buyer did previously own an HDB/EC with subsidy, additional waits apply:

After selling a subsidized HDB or EC, must wait 30 months before applying for any BTO/EC or housing grant
buddy.edgeprop.sg
.

If the buyer canceled a BTO booking in the past, a 1-year wait (and forfeiture penalties) applies from cancellation date
teeco.sg
.

EC specifics: ECs are subsidized hybrid flats – buyers of a new EC are considered to have taken a subsidy. After an EC reaches 5-year MOP, it can be sold to locals (becomes semi-privatized), and after 10 years it’s fully privatized (can be sold to foreigners)
propertyguru.com.sg
. Owners of an EC must also wait 30 months after selling it to apply for a BTO or another EC
buddy.edgeprop.sg
.

Minimum Occupation Period (MOP): HDB buyers must intend to owner-occupy. All HDB flats (BTO or resale) carry a 5-year MOP during which the flat cannot be sold or rented out whole, and owners cannot invest in private residential property
dbs.com.sg
. (If an owner buys a private property during MOP, HDB can compulsorily take back the flat.) New Prime/Plus flats have 10-year MOPs
channelnewsasia.com
. ECs have 5-year MOP before owners can sell or buy a second property. There is no MOP on private properties, but short holding periods incur SSD.

Ethnic Integration Policy (EIP) and SPR Quota: For HDB resale, the app should also check EIP/SPR quota conditions for a given flat (based on buyer’s ethnicity and SPR status relative to the block’s quota). EIP can restrict a resale purchase if the buyer’s ethnic group quota is full in that block (ensuring a diverse mix). The SPR (non-citizen) quota (max 5% or 8% per block) can also bar a resale transaction if the buyers are a non-citizen household and the quota is exceeded. (These conditions vary monthly and by location – the calculator might alert users to check the current EIP/SPR quota for the unit).

CPF Housing Grant Eligibility Logic

Singapore Citizens buying HDB flats as first-timers can get substantial grants. The calculator must determine which grants apply based on buyer profile (citizen status, first-timer status, income, flat type/location).

Key CPF Housing Grants (for HDB purchases):

Enhanced CPF Housing Grant (EHG) – A means-tested grant for first-timer buyers (families or singles) buying either BTO or resale. Household income must be ≤ $9,000 (for families) or ≤ $4,500 (for singles buying alone)
propertyguru.com.sg
propertyguru.com.sg
. The EHG amount is on a sliding scale: lower incomes get more. For example, families earning ≤ $1,500/month get the max $120,000 EHG
propertyguru.com.sg
 (recently raised from $80k
propertyguru.com.sg
), and it tapers down to ~$5,000 at $9,000 income (no grant if income exceeds $9k). Singles get up to $60,000 (half the family amount) at low incomes
propertyguru.com.sg
. Other conditions: At least one applicant must have worked continuously for 12 months, and the flat’s remaining lease must be ≥ 20 years. (If lease doesn’t cover youngest buyer to age 95, the grant might be prorated.) EHG can be used for new or resale HDB, including 2-room Flexi. The app should calculate the exact EHG based on household income input (using HDB’s tier table).

Family Grant (CPF Housing Grant for Resale) – This is for first-timer households purchasing an HDB resale flat (or a resale EC unit) to help with affordability. The amount depends on flat type, citizenship, and income:

For a SC–SC couple buying a resale flat: up to $80,000 for a 4-room or smaller, or $50,000 for 5-room or larger (these are post-2023 increased values)
dbs.com.sg
dbs.com.sg
. These amounts apply if both are SC. If one spouse is PR (SC–PR household), the Family Grant is $10,000 less (e.g. $70k instead of $80k).

Singles Grant: An unmarried SC ≥35 buying a resale flat alone qualifies for half the family grant. That is up to $40,000 (if 4-room or smaller) or $25,000 (5-room or larger) for first-timer singles
propertyguru.com.sg
. (Note: Two single first-timers buying jointly under Joint Singles Scheme can each get Singles Grant, totaling the equivalent Family Grant for the flat.)

Income ceiling for Family Grant is $14,000 (same as HDB flat income ceiling) for families and $7,000 for singles. There is no lower-income tiering for Family Grant – it’s a fixed amount if you qualify as first-timers.

Half-Housing Grant: If only one applicant is a first-timer (e.g. marrying someone who already owns an HDB), a reduced grant (half of the usual amount) may be given. For instance, a couple where one partner is second-timer may get $40k instead of $80k for a 4-room resale (this scenario is subject to HDB approval under the one first-timer, one second-timer scheme).

EC Family Grant: First-timer families buying a brand new Executive Condo (EC) from a developer also get a Family Grant: $30,000 (SC/SC) or $20,000 (SC/PR) if household income ≤ $12,000
propertyguru.com.sg
propertyguru.com.sg
. This grant for ECs tapers down if income exceeds $10k; for example at $11k-$12k income the grant is $10k (SC/SC) or none (SC/PR)
propertyguru.com.sg
propertyguru.com.sg
. If one spouse is second-timer, the EC grant is roughly halved
propertyguru.com.sg
propertyguru.com.sg
. (No grants for EC resale purchases after 5-year MOP, since those are treated as private resales.)

Proximity Housing Grant (PHG) – Encourages families or singles to live with or near their parents/children. Applicable to resale HDB purchases by both first-timers and even repeat buyers. Conditions: The buyers must either 1) live in the same flat/estate as their parents/child, or 2) live within 4 km of their parents’/child’s residence.

For families (or married/engaged couples), PHG is $30,000 if you will live together with your parents/children in the new resale flat, or $20,000 if you live nearby (within 4 km)
madeforfamilies.gov.sg
.

For singles (buying alone), PHG is $15,000 if living with parents, or $10,000 if nearby (half the family amount)
madeforfamilies.gov.sg
.

PHG has no income ceiling. The parent or child must be SC or PR and the intent is genuine proximity living (verified by NRIC addresses). This grant is on top of other grants and even second-timers can get PHG once (if they haven’t before). The app should allow adding PHG if criteria are met, and ensure it’s not already used prior.

Step-Up Housing Grant – A special grant ($15,000) for eligible second-timer families who currently own a small HDB and are upgrading. Specifically, if you currently own a 2-room subsidized flat and are buying a 3-room BTO in a non-mature estate (or a 3-room resale), you may get this one-time grant
propertyguru.com.sg
propertyguru.com.sg
. It’s meant to help those in starter homes upgrade. The app should flag this scenario if current property details are provided.

Grant Combinations: For a resale HDB, a first-timer family can stack Family Grant + EHG + PHG if eligible. E.g. a young couple earning $4,000/month buying a 4-room resale near parents could get Family Grant $80k + EHG $70k
propertyguru.com.sg
 + PHG $20k = $170k total off the price. (Note: total grant is capped by the price if it exceeds; also grants can be used to pay downpayment or reduce loan.) For a BTO, only EHG applies (no Family or PHG since those are for resale). The app’s logic should:

Identify first-timer status for each applicant.

Determine all grants user is eligible for.

Compute grant amounts (based on income and flat specifics).

Ensure that if any applicant has taken grants before, new grants are disallowed (except PHG possible if not taken prior, and Step-Up if applicable).

If one applicant is PR (SC/PR couple), automatically reduce Family Grant by $10k (and note the additional $10k Citizen Top-Up is claimable later if the PR becomes SC)
propertyguru.com.sg
.

Housing Loan Options & Rules

Buyers must decide between an HDB concessionary loan or a bank loan (for HDB purchases), whereas private property purchases can only be financed by bank loans (or other FIs). The app should guide eligibility and financial implications of each:

HDB Concessionary Loan (for HDB purchases only):

Eligibility: At least one buyer must be a Singapore Citizen. Household monthly income ≤ $14,000 (or ≤ $7,000 for singles buying alone) at point of application
dbs.com.sg
. Buyers must not own any other residential property and, if they recently sold one, must wait 30 months (this mirrors HDB flat eligibility)
propertyguru.com.sg
propertyguru.com.sg
. Also, buyers cannot have taken two or more HDB loans previously (generally HDB allows a second loan but imposes conditions on usage of sale proceeds). An HDB Flat Eligibility (HFE) letter will indicate if the buyer qualifies for an HDB loan and the maximum loan amount.

Loan Quantum (LTV): The maximum Loan-to-Value for HDB loans is 75% of the flat price (lower of price or valuation) as of 2024
propertyguru.com.sg
propertyguru.com.sg
. This means a 25% downpayment. HDB loans used to allow 80-85% LTV, but it was tightened to 75% to encourage financial prudence
propertyguru.com.sg
. Important: HDB loan downpayment can be fully paid by CPF OA funds – no minimum cash down (aside from a small option fee). Buyers can choose to pay some cash if CPF is insufficient. (HDB also offers a Staggered Downpayment Scheme for young couples, see Downpayment section below.)

Interest Rate & Tenure: The HDB loan rate is 2.6% p.a. (pegged at CPF OA rate +0.1%) and has remained stable for decades. It’s a fixed rate in practice (not formally fixed, but CPF rate changes are rare). Max tenure is 25 years
cpf.gov.sg
 or up to age 65 of borrower, whichever shorter. HDB loans do not have early repayment penalties.

Mortgage Servicing Ratio (MSR): For HDB loans (and loans for ECs), there is a cap that the monthly mortgage payment cannot exceed 30% of the borrower’s gross monthly income (MSR = 30%)
sg.finance.yahoo.com
. This is calculated using the actual HDB interest rate or a 3% floor rate (MAS mandates a 3% or 4% test; currently 4% stress test is for bank loans
mas.gov.sg
mas.gov.sg
). The HDB loan amount will be capped by MSR if income is lower – the HFE letter already factors this. The app’s loan calculator should enforce MSR: MonthlyPayment(HDBloan) ≤ 0.30 * gross income.

Bank Housing Loan (for HDB or private purchases):

Eligibility: Determined by the banks’ credit criteria, but generally if one does not qualify for HDB loan (e.g. higher income or buying private property), a bank loan is the option. All private property purchases must use bank loans. Bank loans require credit assessment, and an MAS-regulated Total Debt Servicing Ratio (TDSR) of 55% applies
dbs.com.sg
dbs.com.sg
 – meaning total monthly debt obligations (including the new mortgage, car loans, etc.) must not exceed 55% of gross monthly income
dbs.com.sg
dbs.com.sg
. TDSR applies to all property types. For HDB buyers, if opting for a bank loan, they must secure an In-Principle Approval (IPA) from the bank before HDB grants the Option to Purchase.

Loan Quantum (LTV): For a first housing loan, banks can lend up to 75% LTV (assuming loan tenure ≤ 30 years and borrower age such that loan doesn’t extend past 65)
dbs.com.sg
. If the tenure is longer or extends past age 65, a stricter 55% LTV limit applies
dbs.com.sg
. Bank loans also always require a minimum cash downpayment of 5% of the property price
dbs.com.sg
dbs.com.sg
. The remainder of the downpayment can be CPF or cash. For example, first-time private home purchase: 5% cash + 20% CPF/cash, loan 75%.

If the buyer already has an outstanding housing loan (buying a second property), the LTV permitted is much lower (max 45% LTV for the second loan, or 25% if tenure > 30yrs/age 65)
dbs.com.sg
dbs.com.sg
. A third housing loan would be capped around 35% LTV (15% if extended tenure). The app should implement these MAS LTV tiers
dbs.com.sg
.

Note: HDB flats financed with bank loans have a slightly shorter tenure limit (max 25 years) for the 75% LTV; beyond 25 years (up to max 30) or past age 65, LTV drops as above
dbs.com.sg
.

Interest Rate & Tenure: Bank loans have varying interest (floating or fixed for few years). Current rates ~3-4% (subject to market). MAS requires lenders to use a medium-term interest rate floor (currently 4% p.a.) to compute affordability for TDSR/MSR
mas.gov.sg
sg.finance.yahoo.com
. Maximum tenure is 30 years (for residential) or until age ~70, whichever shorter (some banks allow up to age 75 for final repayment, but any tenure beyond 65 triggers lower LTV as noted).

MSR & TDSR: For bank loans on HDB/EC, both MSR 30% and TDSR 55% apply. For private loans, only TDSR 55% applies. The app should apply MSR=30% if property type is HDB or new EC (even with a bank loan)
sg.finance.yahoo.com
. Always apply TDSR=55% for total debt. Use the higher of actual interest or 4% to estimate monthly payment for these ratio tests
mas.gov.sg
.

Loan Decision Logic: The calculator can present a decision flow such as:

If buyer is eligible for HDB loan (SC, income ≤14k, no other property, etc) AND purchasing an HDB flat, then they have a choice: HDB or Bank loan.

Compare pros/cons: HDB loan has higher LTV (75% with 0% cash down) but higher interest (2.6% vs banks ~<2% during low-rate periods – though as of 2025 bank rates ~3-4% so HDB rate is lower or competitive).

Bank loan requires 5% cash and has repayment volatility but can be cheaper if rates fall.

If buyer is not eligible for HDB loan (e.g. an SPR household, or income too high, or already owns a private property until recently), then bank loan is the only option for financing an HDB flat.

For private property, only bank loans are possible. The key check is TDSR: the app should compute allowable loan size based on income and existing debts. For instance, if monthly income is $10,000, total debt payments can’t exceed $5,500 (55%)
dbs.com.sg
dbs.com.sg
; subtract other loans to see what mortgage fits in remaining capacity.

Refinancing rules: If user asks, note that refinancing an HDB loan to bank is one-way (can’t go bank -> HDB loan). Also, second property loans have higher required cash down (at least 25% cash)
dbs.com.sg
dbs.com.sg
.

CPF Usage: CPF Ordinary Account savings can be used for downpayments (except the 5% cash portion for bank loans) and for monthly instalments. For second property purchases, CPF usage is restricted – buyer must set aside the Basic Retirement Sum in CPF first, and only excess CPF can be used
dbs.com.sg
dbs.com.sg
. The calculator should warn if the buyer profile is purchasing a second property without selling the first: they will need significantly more cash/CPF (and pay ABSD, see below). Additionally, CPF cannot be used beyond a property’s valuation limit if LTV <100% (no CPF on portion above valuation). CPF usage on older lease properties is limited if remaining lease < 20 years (prorated if < 40 years remaining).

Financial Costs and Cashflow Timeline

Buying a property incurs various taxes and fees, and the payment schedule depends on property type (BTO vs resale vs private under construction). The calculator should map out the cashflow timeline:

Stamp Duties:

Buyer’s Stamp Duty (BSD) – A tax on all property purchases, based on price or market value. For residential property, BSD rates are tiered:

1% on first $180,000; 2% on next $180,000; 3% on next $640,000; 4% on next $500,000; 5% on next $1,500,000; 6% on any value beyond $3 million
iras.gov.sg
. (These are the latest rates from Feb 2023, with top rate 6% for the portion exceeding $3M
iras.gov.sg
iras.gov.sg
.)

E.g. For a $600k flat: BSD = 1%*180k + 2%*180k + 3%*240k = $5,400 + $3,600 + $7,200 = $16,200. BSD applies to both HDB and private purchases. The calculator should compute this automatically.

Additional Buyer’s Stamp Duty (ABSD) – An extra tax for certain profiles, to curb investment demand. Depends on buyer’s residency and how many properties already owned (counting the new purchase):

Singapore Citizen (SC): 0% ABSD on first property, 20% on second property, 30% on third and subsequent
propertyguru.com.sg
propertyguru.com.sg
.

Permanent Resident (PR): 5% on first property, 30% on second, 35% on third+
propertyguru.com.sg
propertyguru.com.sg
.

Foreigner (any non-PR): a flat 60% on any residential property
propertyguru.com.sg
.

Entities (company/trust): 65% ABSD
propertyguru.com.sg
; housing developers get 35% (plus additional non-remittable 5%)
propertyguru.com.sg
.

These rates were updated on 27 Apr 2023 and remain as of 2025
propertyguru.com.sg
propertyguru.com.sg
. There are limited exemptions (e.g. marriage between SC-foreigner can get ABSD refund for one home, right-sizing seniors can get ABSD refund when selling original property)
propertyguru.com.sg
propertyguru.com.sg
. The app should apply ABSD if the buyer profile triggers it – e.g. a PR buying a resale HDB (first property) will pay 5% ABSD
dbs.com.sg
, an SC upgrading to second home will pay 20%, etc.
dbs.com.sg
.

ABSD is a hefty upfront cost (paid within 14 days of Sale & Purchase agreement). The calculator should highlight this, as it significantly impacts affordability for second-home or foreign buyers.

Other Taxes: If the property is leased out or sold, there could be income tax or GST implications (usually not for typical residential unless buyer is a property trader). Probably out of scope for purchase calculator.

Other Fees:

Legal/conveyancing fees – Typically ~$2,500 to $3,500 for private property conveyancing by lawyers. For HDB, using HDB’s conveyancing service is cheaper (~$1,000+) but many take bank lawyer when using bank loans. The app might include an estimate of legal fees.

Agent commission – If the buyer uses an agent, standard fee is ~1% of purchase price for buyer’s agent (HDB transactions often the seller pays 2% to seller’s agent, and buyer’s agent 1%). Private purchases usually only seller’s agent is involved (2% from seller) and buyer’s agent, if any, gets co-broke from that. The calculator could ask if an agent is used and include ~1-2% cost accordingly.

Valuation fee – For HDB resale, HDB valuation fee ~$120-$200 (if taking HDB loan or to determine how much CPF/grant can be used). For bank loans, valuation by bank’s panel ~ $500.

Stamp fees on loan – A minor fee (mortgage stamp duty) of 0.4% of loan amount (capped at $500) plus some admin, for the mortgage instrument.

Home Inspection/Survey – optional for resale (~$500 if engaged).

Fire Insurance – mandatory for HDB loan (a few tens of dollars)
endowus.com
, and banks usually require a fire insurance too (often covered if you buy mortgage reducing term assurance).

Maintenance Fees – not upfront, but monthly condo maintenance or HDB conservancy charges should be considered in monthly outflows.

HDB BTO Timeline:

Booking/Application: Pay an option fee when selecting a flat: $500 for 2-room, $1,000 for 3/4-room, $2,000 for 5-room/EC. This is paid in cash/NETS and counts toward the downpayment.

Signing Agreement for Lease (about 9 months after booking): Downpayment 10% if taking HDB loan, or 20% if taking a bank loan, is due at this stage

During Construction: Unlike private projects, HDB does not require progressive installment payments. For BTO, no further payments until completion. (If it’s an EC from developer, then progressive payments apply similar to private condo.)

Key Collection (Completion): Pay the remaining downpayment and balance of purchase price. If HDB loan: remaining 15% downpayment (can use CPF) plus any remaining amount not covered by loan (if grants used, they offset price). If bank loan: remaining 5% (making total 25%) must be paid (CPF or cash), and the bank disburses the 75% loan. Legal fees and conveyancing fees are settled, and any refund of excess CPF etc.

Post-Handover: Start of monthly mortgage repayments (HDB loan kicks in immediately after key collection; bank loan typically as well, though some may allow interest-only during construction for ECs). MOP countdown begins from key collection.

HDB Resale Timeline:

House hunting & HFE: Buyer obtains HFE letter (needed before even obtaining an Option to Purchase from seller). Once a flat is found, the buyer and seller negotiate an Option to Purchase (OTP, HDB form).

Option Fee: Pay option fee to seller (capped at $1,000 for HDB resale). This is cash.

Exercise OTP: Within 21 days, buyer confirms purchase by paying the “option exercise fee” (capped such that option+exercise ≤ $5,000) to the seller. So typically, $1k option and $4k exercise (all cash). Upon exercising, both parties submit resale application to HDB.

Submit Intent & HDB Processing: Buyer must have a valid HFE, then submits intent to buy and resale application. HDB approves in ~8 weeks.

Completion (approximately 8 weeks from exercise): At completion meeting, the buyer pays the remaining sum:

Downpayment (if HDB loan: 25% down minus any option fees already paid; if bank loan: 25% down with at least 5% cash already paid as option/exercise).

Use of CPF grants: any grants (Family, EHG, PHG) will be disbursed into the buyer’s CPF and then used toward the flat purchase at completion, reducing cash needed.

Stamp duties: BSD/ABSD must be paid within 14 days of OTP exercise (usually via IRAS e-payment before completion).

Loan disbursement: HDB or bank disburses the loan to seller.

Legal fees: paid (for HDB loan, can be deducted from CPF).

The keys are transferred, and the buyer is now the owner.

After Completion: Start of monthly loan repayments. CPF used will start accruing interest (to be repaid to CPF on sale eventually). Buyer must meet MOP before any future sale or rental of the entire flat.

Private Property (New Launch) Timeline:

Booking Day (Launch): Pay a booking fee (usually 5% of purchase price in cash) to secure the unit. Sign Option to Purchase from developer.

Within ~2-3 weeks: Sign the Sale & Purchase Agreement and pay the remainder of the downpayment: typically 20% (so 15% more since 5% already paid) using CPF and/or cash. Stamp duties (BSD and any ABSD) are due within 14 days of signing S&P – a major cash outlay at this point.

Progressive Payments: As construction reaches milestones, the buyer will be billed for further percentages:

10% upon foundation completion, 10% upon structural framework completion, 5% upon walls completion, 5% on installation of doors/windows, 5% on TOP (temporary occupation permit), 15% on completion date (these are typical stages summing to remaining 75%). Each stage, the bank (or CPF) can pay according to loan proportion. If taking a bank loan, the bank will progressively release loan funds for these payments; the buyer pays interest only on disbursed loan amounts until the full loan is drawn.

TOP/Completion: When the project is completed and closing formalities done, the last payment is made and the buyer gets keys. The loan becomes fully disbursed and full monthly repayments commence.

Post-Handover: No occupancy restriction – can rent out or sell anytime (though SSD applies if within 4 years).

Private Property (Resale) Timeline:

Buyer negotiates and pays an Option fee (often 1% of price) to the seller for an Option to Purchase.

Within typically 14 days, the buyer exercises the OTP by paying an Exercise deposit (often 4% of price, so that total upfront to seller is 5%). These are often cash (or cashier’s order) transactions. A formal Sale & Purchase agreement is signed.

Within 14 days of exercise: Pay BSD and ABSD to IRAS.

Loan and CPF: Secure the bank loan’s Letter of Offer and have CPF ready. Legal conveyancer will handle title search, etc., during an 8-12 week period (standard completion timeline, can be shorter if agreed).

Completion: On completion date, the buyer pays the remaining 95% of price (bank disburses loan up to 75%, CPF funds are released, and any balance cash is paid). Keys are handed over.

Post-completion: The buyer starts the mortgage payments the next month. If it was an owner-occupier purchase, they may be eligible for homeowner fire insurance rates and property tax rates (the app could note to apply for property tax homestead relief).

Monthly Mortgage Calculation:
The app can calculate monthly instalments using standard amortization formula. It should also ensure:

For HDB loans, 2.6% fixed, 25 years max, so roughly $4.53 per month per $1,000 borrowed.

For bank loans, allow input of interest rate and tenure. Possibly include a “stress test” payment at 4% to show what banks use for TDSR calculation.

Incorporate MSR/TDSR: e.g. “Your monthly payment of $X is 25% of income, within the 30% MSR limit” or warn if exceeding.

Advise keeping mortgage servicing below say 25% of income for prudence even if MSR allows 30%.
