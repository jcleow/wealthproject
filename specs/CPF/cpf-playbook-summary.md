### Chapter 2: The 5 Potential Risks Retirees Face

| Risk | Description | Mitigation |
|------|-------------|------------|
| **1. Inflation Risk** | Purchasing power erosion (~2% annually) | Ensure savings earn returns above inflation |
| **2. Longevity Risk** | Outliving retirement savings | Extend working career; each year deferred = ~2 extra years of savings |
| **3. Healthcare Risk** | Rising medical costs (~12% medical inflation in 2025); 1 in 2 age 65+ may become severely disabled | Maintain hospital plan + long-term care insurance |

| **5. Overspending Risk** | [Covered in Part 3] | |

### Key Insight: Sequence of Returns Risk
- For **accumulators** (no withdrawals): sequence doesn't matter - same ending balance
- For **retirees** (with withdrawals): early losses can deplete portfolio completely
- Example: Portfolio A (early losses) ran out at age 81; Portfolio B (early gains) had $2.45M at age 90

### Insurance Premiums Reality Check
- Integrated Shield Plan (private hospital + rider): Average total premium ages 65-100 = **$581,906**
- Compare to ages 0-64: ~$141,601 (about 1/4 of elderly premiums)
- Use CPF Board's Health Insurance Planner to plan

### Chapter 4: Background of CPF

**Timeline**:
- **1955**: British colonial government introduced CPF - mandatory 5% employee + 5% employer contributions
- **2009**: CPF LIFE introduced for lifelong payouts
- **2025**: CPF turns 70 years old

**Evolution**: Started as single account → Now 4 accounts (OA, SA, MA, RA)

---

### Chapter 5: How CPF Accounts Work

| Account | Abbreviation | Purpose | Interest Rate |
|---------|--------------|---------|---------------|
| **Ordinary Account** | OA | Housing, education, retirement, approved insurance | ≥2.5% p.a. |
| **Special Account** | SA | Retirement savings, investment | ≥4% p.a. |
| **MediSave Account** | MA | Medical expenses, insurance premiums | ≥4% p.a. |
| **Retirement Account** | RA | Created at age 55, provides retirement payouts | ≥4% p.a. |

### What Happens at Age 55

**Scenario 1: SA > FRS**
- Transfer FRS amount from SA → RA
- Remaining SA balance → OA (withdrawable)
- SA closes

**Scenario 2: SA < FRS**
- Transfer entire SA → RA
- Transfer from OA → RA until FRS is met
- Remaining OA is withdrawable

**Scenario 3: SA + OA < FRS**
- First $5,000 stays in OA (withdrawable)
- Everything else → RA
- Not required to top up to FRS

**2025 FRS**: $213,000

### Chapter 6: CPF Account Interest Rates

#### Base Interest Rate Calculation

| Account | Formula | Floor Rate |
|---------|---------|------------|
| **OA** | 3-month average of 3 local banks' interest rates | 2.5% p.a. |
| **SA/MA/RA** | 12-month average yield of 10-year SGS + 1% | 4% p.a. |

- Interest rates reviewed **quarterly**
- Interest calculated **monthly**, compounded **annually**
- Credited at the **beginning of the following year**
- Contributions in current month earn interest from **next month**
- Withdrawals in current month **stop earning interest immediately**

#### Additional Interest (Below Age 55)

| Condition | Additional Interest | Cap |
|-----------|---------------------|-----|
| First $60,000 combined (OA+SA+MA) | +1% p.a. | $20,000 from OA |

**Effective rates for below 55:**
- OA (first $20k): **3.5%** (2.5% + 1%)
- SA/MA (remaining $40k of $60k): **5%** (4% + 1%)
- Additional interest on OA credited to **SA**

#### Additional Interest (Age 55 and Above)

| Tier | Combined Balance | Additional Interest | Cap |
|------|------------------|---------------------|-----|
| **Tier 1** | First $30,000 | +2% p.a. | $20,000 from OA |
| **Tier 2** | Next $30,000 | +1% p.a. | $20,000 from OA |

**Effective rates for 55+:**
- OA (first $20k): **4.5%** (2.5% + 2%)
- RA/MA (first $30k): **6%** (4% + 2%)
- RA/MA (next $30k): **5%** (4% + 1%)
- Additional interest on OA credited to **RA**

#### Interest Allocation Priority Order:
1. Retirement Account (RA), including CPF LIFE premium balance
2. Ordinary Account (OA), capped at $20,000
3. Special Account (SA), for those below 55
4. MediSave Account (MA)

### Chapter 7: Basic Healthcare Sum (BHS)

**Definition**: Maximum amount you can have in your MediSave Account, estimated savings for basic subsidised healthcare needs in old age.

**2025 BHS** (for those turning 65 in 2025): **$75,500**

| Age | BHS Behavior |
|-----|--------------|
| Below 65 | Adjusted annually |
| 65 and above | Fixed for life |

#### What Happens When MA Reaches BHS?

**Below Age 55:**
- Excess MA contributions → SA (until SA reaches FRS)
- If SA ≥ FRS → Excess goes to OA

**Age 55 and Above:**
- Excess MA contributions → RA (until RA reaches cohort's FRS)
- If RA ≥ FRS → Excess goes to OA

**Important**: When MA reaches BHS, the additional 1% interest earned on MA is transferred to SA/RA (or OA if SA/RA reached FRS). This differs from SA/RA where additional interest stays in the account.

### Chapter 8: Understanding the Retirement Sums

#### 2025 Retirement Sums

| Sum | Amount (2025) | Multiple | Purpose |
|-----|---------------|----------|---------|
| **BRS** | $106,500 | 1x | Basic living needs, **excluding rent** (assumes property ownership to age 95) |
| **FRS** | $213,000 | 2x BRS | Higher payouts that **include rental expenses** (default) |
| **ERS** | $426,000 | 4x BRS | Maximum top-up for highest payouts |

#### Estimated CPF LIFE Standard Plan Payouts at Age 65

| Retirement Sum | Monthly Payout |
|----------------|----------------|
| BRS ($106,500) | $860 - $930 |
| FRS ($213,000) | $1,610 - $1,730 |
| ERS ($426,000) | $3,100 - $3,300 |

#### Key Rules:

| Aspect | BRS & FRS | ERS |
|--------|-----------|-----|
| When Fixed | Year you turn 55 | Increases every January |
| Transfer Cap (before 55) | OA → SA capped at FRS | N/A |
| Transfer Cap (after 55) | N/A | OA → RA capped at prevailing ERS |

#### Why Retirement Sums Increase Annually:
1. **Inflation**: ~2% average long-term core inflation in Singapore
2. **Longevity**: Life expectancy at 65 increased from 18.0 years (2003) to 21.2 years (2024)
3. **Living Standards**: Higher income leads to higher retirement expectations

#### BRS Derivation (Historical Context):
- 2012/13: Lower-middle retiree monthly expenditure = $518
- Adjusted for 2% inflation to 2016 = $540
- At 65 (10 years later in 2026), need $660/month for same purchasing power
- Requires ~$120,000 at age 65 for CPF LIFE
- Discounted at 4% to age 55 = **$80,500** (first BRS in 2016)
- BRS increases ~3-3.5% annually since then

#### Property Pledge for BRS:
- Can withdraw RA down to BRS (instead of FRS) if:
  - Own property in Singapore
  - Property lease lasts until at least age 95
- Property can cover up to **half** of FRS
- Only **cash component** used for CPF LIFE payout calculation

| Type | Ceiling | Notes |
|------|---------|-------|

| **Additional Wage** | $102,000/year | Bonuses, etc. (formula: $102k - Total OW subject to CPF) |

|-----|----------|----------|-------|

### Strategy 2: Self-Employed Contributions

- **Only MediSave is mandatory** under Self-Employed Scheme
- Required if annual **Net Trade Income (NTI) > $6,000**
- Contribution rate depends on age and NTI
- Must continue contributing even after reaching BHS (excess flows to SA/RA)
- **Tax relief**: Lower of 37% of NTI, $37,740, or actual amount

---

### Strategy 3: Voluntary Contributions to All 3 Accounts (VC3A)

- Top up all 3 CPF accounts up to **CPF Annual Limit ($37,740)**
- Allocation follows your age band rates
- **Key difference from top-ups**: SA allocation still goes to SA even after reaching FRS
- Excess contributions refunded without interest the following year
- Self-employed tax relief: Lower of $37,740 or 37% of NTI

---

### Strategy 4: MediSave Top-Up Only

- Top up MA up to **prevailing BHS**
- **Tax relief**: Up to **$8,000/year** (shared with RSTU)
- Additional **$8,000** for topping up loved ones' MA
- Total potential tax relief: **$16,000/year**

**Loved ones eligible**: Parents, parents-in-law, grandparents, grandparents-in-law, spouse, siblings

**For spouse/siblings tax relief**: They must have annual income ≤ $8,000 or be handicapped

---

### Strategy 5: Retirement Sum Topping-Up Scheme (RSTU)

| Age | Top-Up To | Cap |
|-----|-----------|-----|
| Below 55 | Special Account | Prevailing FRS |
| 55 and above | Retirement Account | Prevailing ERS |

**Tax Relief**:
- Up to **$8,000/year** for self (shared with MA top-ups)
- Additional **$8,000/year** for loved ones
- **No tax relief** for top-ups beyond FRS

**4 Advantages of Reaching BRS/FRS Early**:
1. Can transfer CPF savings to family members
2. Can use OA for multiple properties without housing withdrawal limits
3. Greater liquidity from age 55 onwards
4. Compound interest works for you

---

### Strategy 6: CPF Transfers

| Transfer Type | Cap | When |
|---------------|-----|------|
| OA → SA | Prevailing FRS | Below 55 |
| OA → RA | Prevailing ERS | 55 and above |

**Formula**:
- OA→SA: `FRS - SA balance - Amount withdrawn from SA for investments`
- OA→RA: `ERS - RA balance` (RA balance = principal only, excludes interest/grants)

**Benefit**: Higher interest rate (5% vs 3.5% on first $60k)

**Can also transfer to loved ones' SA/RA** - see transfer rules table in book

### Strategy 6 Trade-offs (continued from Part 5)

**CPF Transfers are irreversible:**
- Cannot use transferred sum for housing
- Reduces RSTU top-up room (less tax relief opportunity)
- No tax relief for transfers
- No MRSS matching grant for transfers

**Recommendation**: Use cash top-ups (RSTU) first for tax relief, then CPF transfers as secondary approach.

### Strategy 7: Voluntary Housing Refund

**Purpose**: Refund CPF used for mortgage back to your OA

**Benefits**:
- Reduces CPF principal used + accrued interest owed
- Get more cash when selling property in future
- Earns risk-free 2.5% p.a. in OA
- **Only strategy to credit money directly into OA**

**After 55**: Refund goes to RA first (up to cohort's FRS), excess stays in OA (withdrawable)

**Housing grants >$30,000**: May be credited to SA/RA and MA, not just OA

### Strategy 8: CPF Investment Scheme (CPFIS)

**Eligibility**: 18+ years old, not undischarged bankrupt, complete Self-Awareness Questionnaire (SAQ)

**Investment Restrictions**:

| Account | Cannot Invest | Can Invest |
|---------|---------------|------------|
| **OA** | First $20,000 | Remainder |
| **SA** | First $40,000 | Remainder |

**Investment Limits (from OA)**:
- Up to **35%** of investible savings: Shares, REITs, Corporate Bonds
- Up to **10%** of investible savings: Gold ETFs, Gold Products

**CPFIS-OA Process**:
1. Open CPF Investment Account (CPF-IA) with DBS, OCBC, or UOB
2. Sale proceeds held in CPF-IA for ~2 months before returning to OA

**CPFIS-SA Process**:
- No separate account needed
- Investments deducted/returned directly from/to SA

**Tax**: Investment gains, interest, dividends are **non-taxable**

**Havend Recommendation**: Discourage investing SA savings - hard to beat risk-free 5% p.a.

**Investment Framework (5 Steps)**:
1. Establish non-negotiable life goals
2. Calculate amount needed
3. Determine need, ability, willingness to take risks
4. Create portfolio asset allocation
5. Invest using low-cost, diversified instruments

### Strategy 9: Matched Retirement Savings Scheme (MRSS)

**Government matches cash top-ups to RA**:
- Up to **$2,000/year**
- Lifetime limit: **$20,000**
- No application needed - auto-assessed yearly

**Eligibility Criteria**:
- Singapore Citizen
- Age 55+ as of 31 Dec
- RA savings < cohort's BRS ($106,500 in 2025)
- Average monthly income ≤ $4,000
- Annual value of residence ≤ $21,000
- Own ≤ 1 property

**Note**: Cash top-ups qualifying for MRSS matching **not eligible for tax relief** (but 100% match > tax benefit)

**From Jan 2026**: Extended to eligible persons with disabilities of all ages

### Chapter 10: Mitigating Longevity Risk with CPF LIFE

**CPF LIFE**: CPF Lifelong Income For the Elderly - national longevity insurance annuity scheme

#### How CPF LIFE Works:
- Pools interest earned on premiums of all CPF members
- Provides **lifelong payouts** even after exhausting own premium
- RA/CPF LIFE savings earn **4-6% p.a.** risk-free

#### Auto-Inclusion Criteria:
1. Singapore Citizen or PR
2. Born 1958 or after
3. Have ≥ $60,000 in RA when payouts start

**No minimum amount** to join voluntarily, but low savings = low payout

#### Payout Timing:
- Eligibility age: **65-70 years old**
- Can defer payouts for up to **7% increase per year**
- Auto-starts at **70** if not initiated
- CPF Board notifies 3 months before age 65

#### Three CPF LIFE Plans Overview:

| Plan | Starting Payout | Bequest | Premium Used |
|------|-----------------|---------|--------------|
| **Standard** (default) | Highest | Lowest | 100% of RA at payout start |
| **Basic** | ~10% lower | Higher | 10-20% of RA; rest stays in RA |
| **Escalating** | Lower initially, increases 2% yearly | Moderate | [Covered in Part 7] |

#### Standard Plan Details:
- **Level monthly payout for life**
- All RA savings → CPF LIFE premium 1 month before payout starts
- Payouts from premium first, then from pooled LIFE fund
- Less bequest (pooled interest not returned to beneficiaries)

#### Basic Plan Details:
- **Level monthly payout for life** (~10% lower than Standard)
- Only 10-20% of RA → CPF LIFE premium
- 80-90% stays in RA, withdrawn as monthly payouts
- From age ~90: Switch to drawing from CPF LIFE premium
- Higher bequest than Standard Plan

### Chapter 10 Summary: Key CPF LIFE Points

**About CPF LIFE**:
- At 55: OA + SA → RA (up to FRS)
- More RA savings = larger CPF LIFE payout
- RA savings used to purchase CPF LIFE
- Designed so members/beneficiaries receive **at least** their premium amount (via payouts and/or bequests)
- Payout eligibility: age **65-70**
- **7% increase per year** of deferment
- Payouts may be adjusted for long-term interest rate or life expectancy changes (expected to be small and gradual)

**Payout Factors**:
1. Amount of RA savings
2. Gender
3. Age
4. CPF interest rate
5. Mortality rates

### Part V: Estate Planning for CPF

### Chapter 11: What Happens to Your CPF Savings When You Pass Away

**Critical**: CPF savings **cannot be distributed via a will**. Must use **CPF Nomination**.

#### Three Types of CPF Nomination

| Type | Method | Key Details |
|------|--------|-------------|
| **Cash Nomination** | Distribute all CPF in cash | **Default option** for all members |
| **Enhanced Nomination Scheme (ENS)** | Transfer to beneficiaries' CPF accounts | Two options available |
| **Special Needs Savings Scheme (SNSS)** | Monthly disbursement for special needs child | Administered by Special Needs Trust Company |

#### ENS Options

**Option 1** (SA/RA Priority):
1. Credit to nominee's SA/RA first:
   - If nominee < 55: up to prevailing FRS
   - If nominee ≥ 55: up to prevailing ERS
2. Excess → Nominee's MA (up to BHS)
3. Remaining balance → Cash

**Option 2** (MA Priority):
1. Credit to nominee's MA first (up to BHS)
2. Excess → Nominee's SA/RA (up to limits)
3. Remaining balance → Cash

#### CPF Nomination Rules

- Nominees can be **persons or organizations**
- Valid nomination requires **2 witnesses** (in person or at CPF Service Centre)
- Cash Nomination can be done **online**
- ENS and SNSS must be done **in person**
- Nominees **under 18**: Public Trustee holds shares until they turn 18

**Important Events**:
- **Marriage**: **Revokes** existing CPF nomination (must make new one)
- **Divorce**: Nomination **remains valid**

**Without nomination**: Distribution follows Singapore's **intestacy laws** or **Muslim Inheritance law**

**Pro Tip URLs**:
- Make nomination: https://www.cpf.gov.sg/makecpfnom
- View nomination: https://www.cpf.gov.sg/viewnomdetails

### OA + SA Shielding Strategy (Before Turning 55)

**Goal**: Deposit idle cash into CPF for higher interest while maintaining liquidity after 55

#### The "Hack":

**Step 1 (Just before 55)**: Use CPFIS to invest amounts above minimum balances
- OA: Keep $20,000, invest the rest
- SA: Keep $40,000, invest the rest
- Invest in low-risk instruments (T-bills, Money Market Funds)

**Step 2 (At age 55)**: RA is created
- Combined $60,000 (OA + SA) < FRS → All goes to RA
- Top up RA with **cash** to reach FRS ($213,000 - $60,000 = $153,000)

**Step 3 (After 55)**: Liquidate investments
- SA is closed → Investment proceeds go to OA
- FRS is met → OA balance is **fully withdrawable**

**Result**: Liquid OA earning 2.5% p.a. risk-free!

#### Example: Yusof (turning 55)

**Before shielding**:
- OA: $258,000 | SA: $247,000 | MA: $75,500

**After investing (before 55)**:
- OA: $20,000 | SA: $40,000 | MA: $75,500
- Invested: $238,000 (from OA) + $207,000 (from SA) = $445,000

**At 55**:
- OA: $0 | MA: $75,500 | RA: $60,000
- Top up RA with $153,000 cash → RA: $213,000 (FRS met)

**After liquidating investments**:
- $445,000 returns to OA (fully withdrawable!)
- RA: $213,000 earning 4% p.a.
- OA: $445,000 earning 2.5% p.a. (liquid)

- Flexible tenure

**Cons**:

- Sequence of returns risk
- Requires investment knowledge
- Not guaranteed

**Pros**:
- Lifelong income (like CPF LIFE)
- Can choose features (death benefit, inflation adjustment)
- No cap on purchase amount

**Cons**:
- Lower payout per dollar vs CPF LIFE
- Subject to insurer's claims-paying ability

## Quick Reference: Key CPF Concepts

### Retirement Sums (2025)

| **FRS** | $213,000 | Includes rental expenses (recommended) |

### CPF Account Types
| Account | Purpose | Interest Rate |
|---------|---------|---------------|
| **OA** | Housing, education | ≥2.5% p.a. |
| **SA** | Retirement (before 55) | ≥4% p.a. |
| **MA** | Healthcare | ≥4% p.a. |
| **RA** | Retirement (after 55) | ≥4% p.a. |

### CPF Interest Rates (Additional Interest)
| Age | First $60k | Effective Rate |
|-----|------------|----------------|
| Below 55 | +1% | OA: 3.5%, SA/MA: 5% |
| 55 and above | +2% (first $30k), +1% (next $30k) | Up to 6% |

### CPF LIFE Plans at a Glance
| Plan | Best For | Starting Payout | Bequest |
|------|----------|-----------------|---------|
| **Standard** | Highest income now | Highest | Lowest |
| **Basic** | Leaving inheritance | ~10% lower | Highest |
| **Escalating** | Inflation protection | ~20% lower (grows 2%/yr) | Moderate |
